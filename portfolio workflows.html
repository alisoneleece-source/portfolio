Operations workflow

Commercial Purchase Order Management

Bringing purchase-order intake, revisions, ownership, and follow-up into a workflow that is easier to track.

The challenge · Before

Information spread across email and attachments

Original purchase orders, revisions, and follow-up messages could be difficult to piece together into a reliable current picture.

Original PO received PDF attached
Revised PO received New version
Follow-up requested Needs review
The solution · After

A structured view of status and next actions

Document history, validation, exception handling, and ownership are brought into one review experience.

PO-1042 · Northwind EnergyNeeds attention
Latest documentRevision 2
Next actionReview amount variance
Assigned ownerCustomer Success

The challenge

Purchase orders arrive through email, often with revised documents and follow-up conversations. Without a clear record of changes, teams have to reconstruct which version is current, what still needs attention, and who owns the next step.

The approach

I mapped the document intake and review process, then worked on a centralized workflow to organize PO details, distinguish revisions, flag exceptions, and make the relevant owner and next action more visible. AI-assisted development supported the prototype and automation work.

Workflow design

01 · INTAKECapture

Receive and organize incoming PO documents.

02 · VALIDATEIdentify

Extract details and flag items requiring review.

03 · TRACKRecord

Maintain visibility into document history and revisions.

04 · ROUTEAssign

Identify exceptions, owners, and approvals.

05 · ACTFollow up

Surface the next step and retain a traceable record.

Designed outcome

A clearer view of what was received, what changed, and which actions remain open, without relying on repeated manual searches through email threads.

Project scope

The linked project is a sanitized, interactive recreation using fictional records. It demonstrates the workflow design and logic rather than a fully deployed system or measured business results.

Internal Knowledge Wiki

Organizing scattered reference material into a more consistent, searchable structure.

The challenge · Before

The information existed. Finding it was the problem.

Answers could be buried across Slack discussions, reference documents, and individual team knowledge.

Team thread Details buried in replies
Reference notes Different locations
Past decisions Hard to retrace
Ask a colleague Interrupts other work
The solution · After

Organized around retrieval

A clear structure makes related guidance easier to discover, revisit, and use.

⌕   Search a topic or process
Relevant guide · Process overview and next steps
Related reference · Supporting detail and context

The challenge

Team knowledge was spread across conversations and documents. Even when an answer already existed, it could take extra searching or repeated questions to locate the right context.

The approach

I worked on gathering and structuring useful reference information into a wiki-style knowledge resource, emphasizing discoverability, consistent organization, and information that people could use independently.

Designed outcome

A more accessible reference point for routine questions, processes, and decisions, so the team could spend less time reconstructing context.

Project scope

The linked example is a sanitized portfolio project intended to demonstrate the approach to organizing and retrieving knowledge. It is not a claim of measured team adoption.
